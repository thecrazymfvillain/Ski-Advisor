Best Race Wax: Fluoro-Free Picks for Ski Racers

Fluorinated wax is banned in FIS racing and most junior and college programs. Here's how to build a fluoro-free race wax kit.

How we picked: we haven't lab-tested these products. The picks come from manufacturer specs and what ski shops and race programs commonly use, and we update the page when models change. As an Amazon Associate, The Ski Advisor earns from qualifying purchases; commissions don't change the picks.

The rules

FIS banned fluorinated waxes from the 2023-24 season, and most US and Canadian junior, college and masters programs follow the same rule. Every wax on this page is fluoro-free. Check your series' rules; some test skis on race day.

Build it in three layers

1. Base layer

A hard, cold wax ironed in first, cooled and scraped. It makes the race wax last on abrasive, injected or man-made race snow. One bar lasts a long time. Shop on Amazon

2. Race wax for the temperature

Fluoro-free race waxes are sold by snow temperature, in the same color bands as the wax chart. Our resort pages name Swix's HS (High Speed) line, from HS5 Turquoise for very cold snow to HS10 Yellow for warm snow, because its colors map cleanly to the chart. Toko, Holmenkol and Vauhti sell fluoro-free race lines by temperature too, and smaller makers such as Wend (MF Race Wax) and mountainFLOW (Race Wax) make plant-based fluoro-free race waxes.

3. Top coat, race morning

A fluoro-free top coat (liquid, block or powder) in the same temperature range, applied on race morning and corked or brushed in per its instructions. Top coats cost the most per use, so most racers save them for important races. Shop on Amazon

How to spend a race budget

  • Training: a durable fluoro-free training wax by the big bar. Re-wax every few training days; injected snow strips wax quickly.
  • Race wax: buy the two temperature bands you race in most, not the whole range. In much of the Rockies that's Blue and Violet; coastal and spring racing leans Red and Yellow. Check your mountain's page for the snow it usually has.
  • Top coats: one, for your most common temperature, saved for races that count.
  • Brushes: keep a separate set for race wax, and never use brushes that touched fluorinated wax.

Cleaning off old fluoro wax

If skis were ever waxed with fluorinated wax, clean them before racing under fluoro-free rules: several hot-scrapes with a soft cleaning wax, scraping while warm each time, and new brushes. More in race wax vs training wax.
