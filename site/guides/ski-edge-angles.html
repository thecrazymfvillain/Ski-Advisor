Ski Edge Angles Explained: 89°, 88° and 87°

What base and side bevel do, how they add up to the number people quote, and which to pick for how you ski.

Two bevels

Base bevel is how far the edge is lifted off the flat of the base, usually 0.5° to 1°. More base bevel means the ski rolls onto its edge more gradually.

Side bevel is how far the side of the edge is filed past vertical, usually 1° to 3°. More side bevel makes a sharper, more aggressive edge that bites harder snow.

Material removed by each bevel is shaded. Angles are drawn five times their real size so you can see them.

What "88°" means

Shops usually describe the side bevel by its angle: a 2° side bevel is called an 88° edge. The actual angle at the corner of the edge also includes the base bevel:

included angle = 90° − side bevel + base bevel

So a ski with 1° base and 2° side has an 89° included angle, even though everyone calls it an 88° edge.

Leave base bevel to the shop

Base bevel is set by a shop's stone grind and you should rarely change it at home. Too much base bevel makes the ski feel vague. At home, only deburr the base edge with one or two light passes in a base guide.

Changing the side bevel

Going from 2° to 3° means filing away more steel, and you can't put it back. Choose an angle and stick with it, and only change it when your skiing changes.

Detuning

Many skiers dull the edge slightly at the tip and tail, past the contact points, so the ski doesn't hook. Racers don't detune. Park skiers detune more.
