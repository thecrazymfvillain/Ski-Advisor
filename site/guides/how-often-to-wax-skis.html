How Often Should You Wax Your Skis?

Most recreational skiers should wax every few ski days. Here's how to tell when your base is dry.

The rule of thumb

Most recreational skiers should wax every three to five ski days. Wax more often on man-made or icy snow, which is abrasive, and on very cold snow. Racers wax for every race and every few training days.

Signs your base is dry

  • White, chalky or fuzzy-looking patches, usually near the edges and underfoot.
  • The skis feel slow on flat traverses or stick in wet snow.
  • The base looks dull even after brushing.

Edges on a different schedule

Edges don't need a full sharpen every time you wax. A quick polish with a fine stone keeps them consistent between full tunes. See how to sharpen ski edges.

New skis

New skis usually come with a factory wax that wears off quickly. A hot wax before the first day is a good habit.

Summer storage

At the end of the season, iron on a thick coat of soft wax and don't scrape it. It protects the base and edges from drying and rust. Store skis somewhere cool and dry, then scrape and brush before your first day.
