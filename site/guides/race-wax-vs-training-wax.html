Race Wax vs Training Wax

Race wax is built for speed on one day. Training wax is built to survive a week of hard laps. Here's how racers use both.

Two different jobs

Training days are about durability. Race courses are usually hard, injected or man-made snow that strips wax quickly, and you're doing many laps. Race day is about speed for a few runs. That's why racers use cheaper, tougher wax for training and save the expensive wax for races.

Training wax

  • A fluoro-free performance wax matched to the temperature. One layer is enough.
  • Re-wax every few training days. Hard training snow wears it off fast.
  • When you're between two waxes, pick the colder, harder one.

Race-day wax, in three layers

  1. Base layer: a hard, cold wax ironed in, cooled and scraped. It helps the race wax last on abrasive snow. Skip it on soft, warm days.
  2. Race wax: a fluoro-free race wax matched to the snow temperature, ironed in, cooled fully, scraped and brushed. Many racers build two or three layers over the week before a race.
  3. Top coat, race morning: a fluoro-free race top coat (block, powder or liquid) in the same temperature range, applied per its instructions and corked or brushed in.

The fluoro ban

Fluorinated waxes are banned in FIS racing, and races test skis for them. If your skis ever had fluoro wax, clean the base thoroughly with several hot-scrapes before racing, and check your club or league's rules.

Edges on race day

Give the side edge a final polish with a fine stone the night before. Most racers run a 3° side edge with 0.5° to 0.7° base. See edge angles explained.
