Waxing for Wet Spring Snow

Warm, wet snow grabs the base with suction. The fix is warm wax, more structure and timing your day.

Why wet snow is slow

When snow is at 32°F (0°C) and wet, a film of water builds between the base and the snow. The base sticks to that film like a wet glass sticks to a table. Spring snow also carries dirt and pollen that cling to the base.

Wax

  • Use a warm (usually yellow) wax rated above freezing.
  • Two thin coats work better than one thick one.
  • For dirty late-season snow, a graphite wax helps resist dirt.

Structure

Base structure is the fine pattern a shop grinds into the base. It channels water away and breaks the suction. Brush hard after waxing to open it back up. Skiers who ski a lot of spring snow sometimes ask the shop for a coarser, warm-snow structure.

Time your day

Spring snow freezes overnight and softens as the sun hits it. Mornings are firm and need sharp edges. Mid-morning corn is the best skiing of the day. Afternoons get slushy. Wax for the afternoon, and keep edges sharp for the morning.

Touch-ups

A rub-on or paste wax in your pocket helps when your skis start to grab in the afternoon.

Corn snow timing by slope direction

Spring snow softens as the sun reaches it. East-facing slopes usually soften first, in the morning; south-facing slopes by late morning or midday; west-facing slopes in the afternoon. North-facing slopes may stay firm all day. Follow the sun around the mountain and you can ski soft corn for hours instead of ice or slush.

Signs your wax is wrong for wet snow

  • The skis feel sticky or grabby, as if braking, especially on flats.
  • They suddenly slow when you move from shade into sun.
  • Wet snow clings to the base or the topsheet.

A rub-on warm wax in your pocket fixes most of this for the afternoon.

Structure for wet snow

The fine pattern ground into the base breaks the suction of wet snow. Brushing restores it after waxing. Skiers who spend a lot of time in spring snow can ask a shop for a coarser, warm-snow structure at their next stone grind.

After the last spring day

Spring snow is dirty and wet, so clean the base with a hot-scrape and dry the edges before storing your skis. Then put on a summer storage coat; see how often to wax your skis.
