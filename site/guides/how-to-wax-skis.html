How to Wax Skis at Home

A full hot wax takes about an hour of work plus cooling time. Here's the whole process, and the mistakes that undo it.

What you need

  • A waxing iron. A dedicated ski iron holds a steady temperature. Clothes irons run hot and uneven, and their steam holes leave stripes.
  • Wax for the temperature you'll ski. See the wax temperature chart, or use a universal wax if you only wax a few times a season.
  • A plastic (plexi) scraper and a nylon brush. Brass and horsehair brushes are worth adding later.
  • Something to hold the ski: a vise, or straps on a sturdy table. A thick rubber band holds the brakes up.

Step by step

  1. Secure the ski base-up. Loop a rubber band over the brake arms so they stay out of the way.
  2. Clean the base. Brush tip to tail. If the base is dirty, hot-scrape it: iron on a soft, warm wax and scrape it off while it's still warm. It pulls dirt out of the base.
  3. Set the iron. Use the temperature on the wax label. Most waxes melt between about 250°F and 300°F (120–150°C). Colder, harder waxes need more heat.
  4. Drip the wax. Hold the bar against the iron and let it drip in a line down each side of the base.
  5. Iron it in. Spread the wax in slow, steady passes from tip to tail. Keep the iron moving. The topsheet should feel warm to the touch, never hot.
  6. Let it cool. At least 30 minutes at room temperature, overnight if you can. Wax keeps soaking in and hardening as it cools.
  7. Scrape. Pull the plexi scraper tip to tail until only fine dust comes off. Clear the edges and sidewalls with the scraper's notch.
  8. Brush. Brush tip to tail until the base shines. For cold, hard wax, start with brass, then nylon. For warm wax, nylon then horsehair.

Mistakes that ruin a wax job

  • An iron that smokes the wax. If you see smoke, the iron is too hot and can damage the base.
  • Stopping the iron in one spot. It can burn or blister the base. Keep moving.
  • Scraping while the wax is warm. You pull the wax back out of the base.
  • Leaving too much on. Unscraped wax makes skis slow and grabby. Scrape until you're sure you're done, then scrape again.
