How to Wax a Snowboard at Home

Waxing a snowboard works like waxing skis, on one wide base. Here's the whole process, and how board edges differ.

What you need

  • A waxing iron and wax for the temperature you'll ride. Ski and snowboard wax are the same product; see the wax temperature chart.
  • Something to hold the board: a snowboard vise, or two padded supports on a bench, one under each binding.
  • A plexi scraper and a nylon brush. A wide scraper saves time on a board.
  • Edge tools if you want to sharpen: a side edge guide, diamond stones and a gummy stone.

Step by step

  1. Set the board up base-up on padded supports or a vise. The bindings can stay on.
  2. Clean the base. Brush nose to tail. If it's dirty, hot-scrape it: iron on a soft, warm wax and scrape it off while it's still warm.
  3. Set the iron to the temperature on the wax label.
  4. Drip the wax in a zigzag across the whole base. A board needs roughly two to three times as much wax as a ski.
  5. Iron it in with slow, overlapping passes from nose to tail. Keep the iron moving, and check the topsheet stays warm, never hot.
  6. Let it cool for at least 30 minutes, overnight if you can.
  7. Scrape nose to tail until only fine dust comes off, and clear the edges and sidewalls.
  8. Brush nose to tail until the base shines and the structure shows again.

Snowboard edges

Most snowboards come from the factory with a 1° base bevel and a 1° side bevel (an 89° edge). Riders who carve on firm snow often prefer a 2° side bevel; park riders keep 1° and detune more. Like ski edges, the angle is a one-time setup. Keep your board's angle and change how sharp you keep it: polish before firm days, deburr before soft ones.

Detuning a board

Many riders lightly dull the edge near the nose and tail contact points, where the base starts to lift, so the board doesn't catch when sliding or riding rails and boxes. Freestyle riders detune more; freeriders and carvers detune little or not at all. Never detune the edge underfoot unless you ride mostly park features.

How often

Every three to five riding days for most riders, sooner on man-made or very cold snow. White, dry-looking patches near the edges mean the base is overdue. Our resort pages pick the wax for your mountain and show the whole tune for a snowboard when you choose Snowboard.
