Or buy a bundled kit

Many kits in the $50–100 range bundle an iron, wax, scraper, brushes and an edge tool. They're a reasonable start. Before you buy one, check:

  • The iron has temperature markings and no steam holes. This is where cheap kits cut corners.
  • The edge tool lists its angle, ideally 88° (a 2° side bevel), or adjusts to it.
  • The wax is fluoro-free, and there's enough of it. A snowboard uses far more wax than a pair of skis.
  • The brush is real nylon, not a stiff plastic pad.

Snowboards

The same kit works. Buy a bigger wax bar, since a board's base is much wider, and use padded supports under each binding instead of straps.

Is it worth it?

A shop wax usually costs about as much as a bar of wax and a scraper. If you ski more than a handful of days a season, a home kit pays for itself in the first season, and your skis get waxed more often.
