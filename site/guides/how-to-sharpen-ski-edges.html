How to Sharpen Ski Edges at Home

Sharp edges grip ice and hard groomers. Here's how to sharpen them with a guide and diamond stones without wrecking the bevel.

What you need

  • A side edge guide at your angle (most skiers use 2°, an 88° edge). See edge angles explained.
  • Diamond stones in 200, 400 and 600 grit. A coarser stone helps with rock damage.
  • A gummy stone for deburring and detuning.
  • A file, only if you're setting a new angle or repairing serious damage.
  • A vise or straps and a permanent marker.

Step by step

  1. Check the edges. Run a fingernail along them. Burrs catch your nail. Rock hits leave hardened spots that a file skates over. Knock those down with a coarse stone first.
  2. Mark the edge. Run a marker along the side edge. After a few passes, the ink shows exactly where you're removing steel and confirms the guide is sitting flat.
  3. Deburr the base edge. One or two light passes with a fine stone in a base guide. Don't sharpen the base edge.
  4. Sharpen the side edge. Wet the stone, set it in the guide and work tip to tail in long, overlapping strokes. Go coarse to fine: 200, then 400, then 600 grit. Give each edge the same number of passes.
  5. Deburr. One light pass with a gummy stone along the base and side edge.
  6. Detune if you need to. Dull the tip and tail past the contact points if your skis hook. Racers skip this.

How often

A quick polish with a fine stone every few ski days keeps edges consistent. Do a full sharpen after rock hits, or when your skis start sliding out on firm snow. On icy, eastern or man-made snow, sharpen more often.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Pressing hard. Let the stone do the work. Pressure rocks the guide and rounds the edge.
  • Uneven passes. One edge sharper than the other makes the ski behave differently turning left and right.
  • Filing the base edge. It changes the base bevel and the ski feels vague. Leave that to the shop.
