Tuning for Ice and Man-Made Snow

On hard, icy and machine-made snow, sharp edges do most of the work, and your wax needs to get harder too.

What makes it different

Man-made snow has small, dense, rounded grains. Packed down, it turns hard and icy, and it's abrasive on bases and edges. Freeze-thaw cycles make natural snow icy too, which is why eastern skiing is so hard on skis.

Edges

  • A 2° side edge (88°) is the minimum for regular ice. Strong skiers often run 3° (87°).
  • Sharpen often. A quick polish with a fine stone before each ski day makes a real difference.
  • Deburr after every sharpen, and fix rock hits quickly.

Wax

  • Go one color colder (harder) than the temperature alone suggests. Soft wax wears off quickly on abrasive snow.
  • Re-wax more often. On man-made snow, the base dries out in a few days.
  • A hard base-layer wax under your regular wax improves durability.

Our resort pages automatically suggest sharper edges and harder wax when the forecast points to firm, refrozen or man-made snow.

Why machine-made snow behaves differently

Natural snowflakes are light, branching crystals. Machine-made snow is made of small, dense, rounded ice grains. Groomed, it packs into a hard, abrasive surface that holds an edge well when it's fresh and turns icy after freeze-thaw cycles or heavy traffic. It also wears wax and dulls edges faster than natural snow.

Early season

Early-season coverage is often thin, so rocks and debris are more likely. Many skiers use an older pair of skis until the base builds up. Expect more edge damage, and check your edges after each day.

A tuning routine for an icy week

  1. Before the trip: a full side-edge sharpen at your existing angle and a hard, cold wax.
  2. Each evening: a quick polish with a fine diamond stone and a gummy stone pass.
  3. Every two or three days: re-wax, because abrasive snow dries the base out quickly.

What not to do

Don't sharpen the base edge to get more grip, and don't change your side angle for a single trip. Both remove steel you can't put back. A sharp edge at your usual angle grips far better than a dull one at a steeper angle.
